🌄 Phase 1 — Morning · Ryokan Breakfast
07:1575 min
Japanese Breakfast — The Ryokan Morning BreakfastIncl.Incl.
The second part of your half-board: a full Japanese breakfast served in the ryokan. This is the meal many Western visitors remember as unexpectedly moving — a considered sequence of small dishes, more satisfying than it first appears.
🍳 Typical ryokan breakfast: steamed rice · miso soup · grilled fish (salmon or mackerel) · tamagoyaki egg · pickled vegetables · tofu · nori seaweed · seasonal side. Eat slowly. It sets the tone for the whole day.
⏰ Confirm breakfast start time at check-in yesterday — some Laforet rooms serve from 07:00, others from 07:30 or 08:00. If 07:00 isn't available, adjust the rest of the morning forward by 30 min — the ropeway opening time is the only fixed point.
🗻 Before breakfast: check hakonenavi.jp (ropeway status) AND look out a north-west-facing window from the ryokan — if Fuji is visible this morning, it won't be later. Ropeway suspension due to volcanic gas is not uncommon; the bypass is direct bus Gora → Togendai.

Sounzan → Owakudani — Ropeway ⭐ PeakFree PassCovered
One of the great mountain experiences in Japan — gondolas crossing the volcanic ridge with Owakudani's steam vents visible below and, on clear mornings, Mt Fuji in full profile to the north-west.
🗻

Mt Fuji — Visibility at 09:00

August mornings give the best odds of seeing Fuji from the ropeway — typically visible until 10:00–11:00 before afternoon cloud builds around the summit. From the gondola between Sounzan and Owakudani, the mountain appears to the north-west (to the right looking forward from Sounzan). Don't be disappointed if it's hidden — August cloud is common, and the ropeway experience is complete regardless.

🚡 The gondolas are small (4–6 people) and move continuously — don't wait for an empty one, board as soon as you reach the platform. Sounzan → Owakudani is ~15 minutes. Standing gives better views through the floor-to-ceiling windows than sitting.
09:2045 min
Owakudani — Volcanic Valley + Kuro-tamago 🥚 Volcanic¥500~A$5
Owakudani is an active geothermal zone — steam vents, sulphur deposits, the distinct smell of eggs in the air. Since the 2015 volcanic activity, the full Valley of Great Boiling trail is closed; you walk the shorter viewing loop around the main building, which still gives genuine proximity to the vents without entering restricted zones.

Buy a bag of kuro-tamago (black eggs — ¥500 for 5 eggs). Hard-boiled in the volcanic spring; the sulphur turns the shells black and the whites slightly grey. The taste is essentially a normal hard-boiled egg — folklore says each one adds 7 years to your life. Eat them here at the source.
⚠️ Respiratory sensitivity note: if either of you has asthma or strong sulphur sensitivity, stay inside the main building rather than walking the viewing loop. The sulphur concentration outside is real — most people are fine, but it can trigger coughing. The eggs are sold inside; no need to go near the vents.
10:0525 min
Owakudani → Togendai — Ropeway Continues RopewayFree PassCovered

⛴️ Phase 3 — Lake Ashi · Choose Your Afternoon
10:30Flex
Lake Ashi — Decision Point ChooseFree PassCovered
Lake Ashi is a volcanic crater lake — deep blue-green, forested mountains, potential Fuji reflection on clear mornings. Fuji is visible from the southern shore looking back north-west across the water — the classic torii-gate-with-Fuji shot requires being at Moto-Hakone looking back toward Togendai direction.

Decide at Togendai based on how the morning has felt. The ropeway + Owakudani took 90 minutes. August heat accumulates. Be honest about energy levels here.

10:30

11:00

11:55

⚠️ Bus risk in August: Hakone buses are the weakest link in the Free Pass network — tourist crowds, summer heat, limited standing room. If a bus arrives full, next one is 20 minutes away. The Tozan train or a ¥3,000 taxi eliminates this entirely. The taxi is worth the money today.
🎨 Phase 4 — Afternoon · Hakone Open-Air Museum
13:302 hrs
Hakone Open-Air Museum ⭐ Art¥4,000~A$41
One of Japan's finest outdoor sculpture parks — 70,000 square metres of mountain landscape with works by Rodin, Henry Moore, Miró, Calder, and a dedicated Picasso pavilion. Admission ¥2,000pp. The combination of large-scale sculpture with the Hakone mountains as backdrop is genuinely extraordinary.
☀️ Heat strategy for today specifically: the Open-Air Museum is largely outdoor and exposed. 2 hours in August afternoon sun is punishing. The correct sequence is indoor spaces first while you're still cool, outdoor sculpture last with foot onsen breaks. Don't do the Henry Moores first — you'll be too hot to enjoy the Picassos.
🎨 Open-Air Museum — Heat-Adapted Walk Sequence
🎭
Picasso Pavilion (INDOOR, air-conditioned) — go here first. 300 Picasso works in a dedicated building, cool inside. The ceramic plates and glass works are the most interesting. 30 minutes.
🗼
Symphonic Sculpture / Stained Glass Tower (INDOOR) — a hollow tower lined entirely with stained glass mosaics. Cool inside, extraordinary light effects. 15 minutes. 14:30 afternoon light is best.
♨️
Foot onsen (OUTDOOR but cooling) — free outdoor foot bath mid-park. Sit, soak your feet, cool down before continuing. Bring a small towel (or rent one ¥100). 20 minutes. This is the pivot between indoor and outdoor.
🧍
Henry Moore Collection (OUTDOOR) — the largest Henry Moore collection in Asia. Reclining figures in the mountain landscape. Walk slowly. 30 minutes. Keep to shaded paths where possible.
🎨
Remaining outdoor sculptures — self-edit — Rodin, Miró, Calder and others scattered through the grounds. Do what your energy allows; exit whenever you've had enough. No requirement to see everything.
🚞 The museum is a 1-minute walk from Chokoku-no-Mori Station on the Hakone Tozan railway — which runs back to Gora. Take the train back rather than the bus: slower, more atmospheric, and the correct way to end a Hakone day.
♨️ Phase 5 — Evening · Second Onsen · Final Kaiseki
16:302 hrs
Second Onsen — The Afternoon Bath ♨️ OnsenIncl.Incl.
The second onsen of your stay — this time after a full day of outdoor activity. Mineral water at 41°C on tired legs and feet after ropeway, lake ferry, and sculpture park is the correct reward. Use the rotenburo (outdoor bath) if available — the late-afternoon light on the mountain cedar is beautiful.
💡 The foot onsen at the museum was a preview. The full onsen is the main event. Drink a glass of water before entering. Stay 12–15 minutes, exit slowly. The minerals stay in your skin for hours — don't shower immediately after.
