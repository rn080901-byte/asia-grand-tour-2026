08:302.5 hrs
Peace Memorial Museum ⭐⭐ Essential¥1,000~A$10
The most important place on this 42-day trip. ¥200pp admission + ¥300pp audio guide — budget ¥1,000 total for two including guides. Allow the full 2.5 hours. Go at whatever pace feels right — there is no correct speed through here, only an honest one.
🕊️ How to Move Through the Museum
📐
East Building first — historical context: why Hiroshima, what happened on 6 August 1945, the science of the bomb. This grounds everything that follows. The audio guide (¥300pp) is worth it; it pulls in survivor voices the signage alone doesn't carry.
🕯️
Main Building — personal stories — this is the part that stays with you. Clothing, personal belongings, the shadows burned into stone, survivor testimonies. Sadako's paper cranes. Walk slowly. Read the small print on the exhibits.
🌅
The panoramic window at the end — the museum is oriented so the final exhibit hall frames the A-Bomb Dome through a wide horizontal window, with the rebuilt city behind it. The message is unmissable.
🪑
Take breaks if needed — seating is provided mid-way through. The museum is emotionally heavy; sitting for a few minutes is completely normal here. There is no requirement to push through.
📖
National Peace Memorial Hall (free, adjacent) — after the main museum, the underground memorial hall. A circular space with names and photographs of victims. Small, profound, often empty. Worth 15 minutes.
🕊️ Yesterday you stood outside and let the space speak. Today the museum gives it language. The combination — outdoor grounds then interior museum — is by design more powerful than either alone. You will leave this building changed in some small but real way. That is the point.
~11:0030–45 min
Quiet Walk + River Pause Decompress¥0Free
Exit when you're done — not at a set time. If the museum takes 3 hours instead of 2.5, that is correct. This is the one place on the trip where a schedule does not apply.

Walk to the Motoyasu River bank. Sit for 15–20 minutes on the stone steps near the A-Bomb Dome. Don't go straight to the tram. The museum needs open air before the next thing.

You don't need to talk much. Just sit. Then when you're ready — around 12:00 — walk or tram to Hiroshima Station for the JR to Miyajima.
⏱️ No pressure on the Miyajima ferry departure. Trains to Miyajimaguchi run every 15–20 min, ferries every 10–15 min. Leave when you're ready — even a 12:30 departure gives you 4+ hours on the island.

⛩️ Phase 3 — Miyajima · Afternoon
~12:453.5 hrs
Miyajima Island — Three Anchors ⭐ Peak Experience¥400 + food~A$4 + lunch
One of the three official "Views of Japan" (Nihon Sankei). Sacred island, free-roaming deer, the great torii of Itsukushima, and one of the oldest shrines built over water in the country. Daisho-in has been cut from today — on this emotionally heavy day, in August heat, after a 2.5-hour museum, adding a vertical temple climb is the wrong trade. The island gives you plenty without it.
🌊
Tide note for 18 August 2026: predicted low tide in Hiroshima Bay falls across most of your afternoon window. This means the torii will not appear to float today — it'll be standing on exposed mudflat, and you can walk directly to its base and touch the wood. This is a different experience to the classic image — and in many ways more remarkable. Most visitors never get to do it. Confirm live tide times tonight at tide-forecast.com/miyajima before you sleep.
⛩️ Miyajima — Low-Tide Walk Sequence
Arrive
First view from the ferry terminal beach. Walk west along the waterfront. The torii appears immediately, planted on the exposed mudflat. Stand here. Sit and look at it for 5 minutes without a phone. Let the scale register.
13:00
Walk to the torii base — while the tide is out, you can walk straight across the mudflat to stand beside the gate. The wood is 600 years of salt and weather. Touch it. Take one photograph. The scale up close is different from the postcard view.
13:30
Itsukushima Shrine (¥300pp · combo with treasure hall ¥500pp). Even at low tide the wooden corridor over the mudflat has a different, more architectural beauty — you see the stilts and construction that are usually hidden underwater. Walk the full length slowly.
14:15
Anago-meshi lunch at Fujitaya or equivalent — conger eel over rice, the island's signature dish. Queue expected but moves. ~¥3,000pp for the full meal set. Eat near the waterfront in the shade. This is the correct Miyajima lunch.
15:30
Omotesando shopping street — 10 min wander. Momiji manju (maple-leaf cakes, red bean or custard), knife-makers, oyster stands. Buy a warm bag of manju for the ferry home. Deer will try to eat your map — they're designated national treasures and shameless thieves.
16:00
One more look at the torii from the western shore, then back to the ferry terminal. The light is softer now. The island quiets as the day visitors leave.
~16:30
Ferry back — stand on deck for the departure view. The torii receding as you cross. Wave at it. You'll likely never see it again in this lifetime; give it a moment.
☀️ August heat note: Miyajima's waterfront walks are exposed. Drink water in the shade, not while walking. If either of you is flagging by 15:30, come back early — there's no ceremony about ferry departure times. The island's gift is the torii and the shrine. Everything after that is optional.
🦌 The deer are sacred and shameless. Keep bags zipped. They will eat guidebooks, paper maps, receipts, anything within reach. Let them approach rather than chasing them for photos. Do not feed them — it's illegal, and also they'll follow you for an hour.
~16:3045 min

18:3090 min
Hiroshima Oysters — Simple Evening Dinner¥6,000–10,000~A$62–103
Hiroshima is Japan's largest oyster producer — ~60% of national output comes from Hiroshima Bay. Tonight is for oysters, simply prepared, with cold Sapporo. No elaborate cuisine. After the museum and Miyajima, simple is right.

How to pick a place: walk the Hondori / Nagarekawa area (10 min from the hotel by tram or taxi). Look for places with "kaki-tei" (牡蠣亭) or "kaki ryori" (牡蠣料理) signage — Hiroshima oyster specialists. Counter seating, visible kitchen, locals after work. Several good options within two blocks of each other.

Order a mixed plate: raw, grilled with ponzu, deep-fried (kaki furai). ¥300–500 per oyster depending on preparation. Add Hiroshima tsukemen or yakiniku sides if still hungry.
🦪 Hiroshima oysters are meatier, more mineral, and larger than Sydney rock oysters — peak season is winter, but August oysters are still good. The hotel concierge can name two or three current specialists within walking distance; ask rather than commit to a name online.
