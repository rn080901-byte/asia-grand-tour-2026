Arashiyama Bamboo Grove ⭐⭐ Peak
¥0 Free

The bamboo towers 20 metres on both sides of the path. The light at 07:15 filters through in green shafts, cool and otherworldly. The sound is soft rustling and distant birdsong. At this hour you'll have long stretches entirely to yourselves.

The grove itself is only about 400m long — don't rush through. Walk in, walk back, walk in again. Different angles at different points in the morning light. The texture of the bamboo trunks, the sound, the green — this is one of the defining sensory experiences of the trip.

🎋
By 10:00 this path is a solid crowd and the shafts of light are gone. By 11:00 it's essentially impassable. You are here at exactly the right hour — use it. Sit on one of the low stone walls. Listen.
📸
The classic shot is looking straight up the path with the bamboo forming a green tunnel overhead. A portrait-orientation phone shot works better than landscape here. Stand still for 30 seconds before taking it — this is not a photo to hurry.

Tenryu-ji is directly adjacent to the bamboo grove — in fact, the grove sits on the temple's former grounds. Short walk to the north entrance. Tenryu-ji garden opens 08:30; you'll arrive right at opening.

08:30
75 min
Tenryu-ji Zen Garden ⭐ Garden
¥1,000 ~A$10

UNESCO World Heritage garden. One of Japan's finest pond gardens, designed in 1339 by the monk Muso Soseki — the shakkei ("borrowed scenery") composition uses the Arashiyama mountains behind as part of the design itself.

You arrive at opening. The garden is at its most serene in the first hour. Walk the full pond circuit slowly. Sit on the garden-side veranda if benches are available.

🪷
Garden-only ticket (¥500pp). This is the right call — 75 minutes outside in the garden is the experience. The building interior (+¥300pp) adds tatami rooms that are lovely but not essential; skip in August heat.
⏰
By 09:30 tour groups will begin arriving. Be out of the main pond viewing area by then — the back paths and smaller sub-gardens stay quieter for longer.

☕

15:00
2 hrs
Kinkaku-ji — The Golden Pavilion ⭐ Iconic
¥1,000 + matcha ~A$10 + tea

The gold-leaf Zen temple reflected in the mirror pond — the most photographed building in Japan. Yes, it's busy on a Sunday. That doesn't matter. It is extraordinary regardless, and the afternoon light at 15:00–17:00 falls on the gold facade from the west, creating a warm reflection that harsh midday sun doesn't produce.

Today you have no second destination — Kinkaku-ji is the only stop of the afternoon. Use the time properly.

📍 Kinkaku-ji — How to Spend Two Hours (one-way path)
15:00 Entry gate + first viewpoint. Admission ¥500pp. IC cards and QR now accepted (2024+); ¥2,000 cash is fine as backup. The main viewing platform is left of centre — pavilion reflected in Kyoko-chi pond. Take time composing the shot.
15:30 Walk the garden circuit. The path is one-way — you cannot come back to the main viewpoint after this. Proceed slowly. Stone lanterns, koi pond, bronze phoenix details on the rooftops visible from below.
16:00 Upper garden — Anmintaku Pond with its island shrine, and the Gin-gado pavilion. Most visitors rush past these — they are the quieter heart of the complex.
16:30 Sekkatei tea pavilion (near exit). Sit down. Matcha and a wagashi sweet (¥500pp). This is the right pause — you've earned it, the light outside is at its golden late-afternoon best, and you're about to exit. Do not try to double back to the main viewpoint; the path doesn't allow it.
17:00 Exit. A last look at the pavilion through the trees as you leave. The afternoon light on the gold is at its richest now.
💡
The first and third floors are gold leaf; the second is natural lacquered wood. Three different architectural periods (Shinden, Samurai, Zen), one building. Unusual and intentional — the pavilion was designed as a statement about the harmony of these three styles.
17:00
60 min
Taxi Home + Slow Prep Prep
~¥2,500 ~A$26

Taxi from Kinkaku-ji back to the hotel — ~20 minutes, no bus stress. This is the right call on the last Kyoto evening.

Shower properly. Take your time dressing for dinner. This is your final night in one of the world's great cities — the pace of the evening should reflect that.

🌊 PHASE 4 · KAWAYUKA DINNER · LAST KYOTO NIGHT
18:30
2.5 hrs
Pontocho Kawayuka — The Platform Dinner ⭐ Finale
¥30,000–50,000 ~A$309–515

5-minute walk from the hotel. Kawayuka (川床) platforms extend from Pontocho restaurants out over the Kamo River between May and September — you literally dine above the water. This is the Kyoto meal you'll remember.

How to book Hotel concierge at Mitsui Garden. Ask this afternoon when you wake from the rest. Pontocho kawayuka platforms typically require 2+ weeks' notice — but concierges have relationships and can sometimes get same-day tables from cancellations. Tell them your budget range.
Realistic pricing ¥15,000–25,000pp for kaiseki on a kawayuka platform is normal. ¥12,000pp is the floor; ¥20,000pp is the sweet spot. Budget ¥30,000–50,000 total for two with drinks and service.
Style Most kawayuka dinners are Kyoto kaiseki — a prix-fixe multi-course progression with seasonal ingredients. Some platforms serve yakiniku or Chinese-Kyoto fusion. Ask the concierge which style you prefer.
If no kawayuka If nothing is available, a regular (non-platform) Pontocho kaiseki dinner still delivers — ¥8,000–15,000pp. The river view isn't above the water but the experience of the narrow alley and the machiya room is fully intact.
🌊
After dinner, walk down to the Kamo River embankment. The illuminated Pontocho lanterns reflect on the water, and the Higashiyama mountains are dark silhouettes to the east. Sit on the stone steps. You've had three nights in one of the world's great cities. Let the last one settle properly.
