Tsukiji Outer Market — Breakfast Graze ⭐ Breakfast¥6,000–10,000~A$62–103
The outer market (always open to public) surrounds the former wholesale fish market site. Since the wholesale market moved to Toyosu in 2018, the outer market has thrived as the eating-on-the-spot destination. Most stalls open 06:00–14:00 with variation by vendor; Saturday is a good day (unlike Sunday when many stalls close).

The strategy: walk the market once end to end, then go back and eat. See what smells extraordinary, what has a queue of locals. Don't commit to the first thing you see.
🐟 Tsukiji Graze — What to Eat
Tuna sashimi at an outer-market counter — fresh bluefin, eaten standing. Look for a counter with a queue of Japanese office workers on a Saturday morning — that's the sign. Most of the famous old inner-market sushi counters (Sushi Dai, Daiwa) moved to Toyosu in 2018 — outer-market options are very good and much easier to access.
¥1,000–2,000
Tamagoyaki on a stick — sweet rolled egg skewers sold warm from the pan. Tsukiji tamagoyaki is distinctively sweeter than elsewhere — a Tokyo-style speciality.
¥150–300
Uni don (sea urchin on rice) — if available. Creamy, oceanic, complex. One small bowl. The flavour that defines Tsukiji.
¥1,200–2,000
Grilled scallops or oysters — cooked in the shell over charcoal, ¥300–500 each. Briny, hot, eaten standing.
¥600–1,000
Canned coffee from a vending machine — the unofficial closing ritual. Warm or cold. Stand on the pavement, watch the vendors pack up. That is Tsukiji morning.
¥150
💴 Cash only at most stalls. Bring ¥8,000–10,000. Eating while walking is acceptable here (unlike Kyoto's Nishiki). By 09:30 the tourist crowds are heavy — leaving by 08:45 gives you the market at its best.

TeamLab Planets — Immersive Digital Art ⭐⭐ Pre-booked¥7,600–8,800~A$78–91
Multiple immersive environments across three themed zones (Water, Garden, Forest — with new spaces added in 2023 and 2024). Barefoot throughout. Some rooms with water up to the knee. Environments respond to your presence — flowers bloom around your feet, koi scatter as you walk, mirror universes extend infinitely in every direction.

This is not a gallery. You are inside the artwork.
🌊 TeamLab Planets — Key Spaces
💧
Water Area — wade through shallow water with projected koi around your feet. The fish scatter and reform around your body. Real water, knee-deep in places. Shoes off before entering; free lockers provided.
🌸
Floating in the Falling Universe of Flowers — flowers bloom, die, and fall around you in a large mirrored space. Most visitors describe this as the defining TeamLab moment.
💎
Crystal Universe — mirrored room with LED crystal structures extending infinitely. Stand inside, turn slowly. One of the most photographed rooms in the exhibition.
🌿
Moss Garden (Garden Area) — softly lit moss spheres that glow and ring a tone when touched, triggering responses across the garden. Quiet, meditative.
🌳
Forest Area (added 2023) — ecosystems of digital creatures that interact across multiple rooms. Newer, less photographed, often less crowded than the classic spaces.
👗 Dress code note: legs will get wet in the Water Area. TeamLab provides complimentary shorts for anyone wearing long pants or skirts that would get wet — available at the entrance. You don't need to dress specifically; they'll lend you what you need.
📱 Phones allowed throughout and the environments are photogenic — but spend at least half the time without a phone in your hand. Rooms are designed to be experienced, not documented. Better photographs come from putting the phone down first, then picking it up.
✅ Before trip: verify the venue is still operating for your date — TeamLab's Toyosu location has announced future relocation plans. Check teamlabplanets.com for current status and booking.

🛏 Phase 3 — Midday Rest · Saturday Heat

Deep Rest + Late Lunch in Room Rest¥1,500~A$15
Grab a simple konbini lunch (Family Mart or 7-Eleven near the hotel) and eat in the room — onigiri, a bento, cold drinks. Don't seek out a restaurant in the heat.

Shower, aircon 24°C, lie down. 500ml water. This is not laziness — it's the engine of the evening. Saturday in Tokyo in August is the absolute peak of the city's heat; 35°C feels like 42°C in the afternoon sun. Any afternoon program without a proper rest block produces a wife who cannot enjoy Asakusa at dusk or Omoide Yokocho at night.
☀️ Tokyo August heat is extreme — more oppressive than Kyoto or Osaka due to the urban heat island effect of 14 million people and their air conditioning exhausting heat into the streets. Alarm at 15:30. Depart for Asakusa by 16:00.

Senso-ji + Asakusa at Dusk ⭐ Old Tokyo¥0Free
Tokyo's oldest temple, founded 628 AD. The contrast with modern Tokyo is complete — incense smoke, lanterns, paper fortunes tied to wire racks, the great five-storey pagoda. Arriving at dusk is the deliberate choice — the midday crowds have thinned, the air has cooled slightly, and the lanterns begin to light as the sun sets (~18:10 on 22 Aug). Golden hour on the temple complex is extraordinary.
⛩️ Asakusa Walk Sequence — Dusk Version
🏮
Kaminarimon Gate (16:30) — the giant red lantern. The photograph everyone takes. Take it, then look up at the underside — a carved dragon facing upward that most visitors miss entirely.
🛍️
Nakamise-dori (16:45) — the 250m covered shopping lane to the main hall. Most shops close 18:00–19:00, so you have a narrow window. Buy ningyo-yaki (small sponge cakes shaped like the temple) and a paper fortune (omikuji ¥100). If the fortune is bad, tie it to the wire rack and leave it behind — that is the correct custom.
⛩️
Senso-ji Main Hall (17:15) — stand at the giant incense cauldron. Fan the smoke toward you; it is said to bestow health wherever it touches. The hall itself is free to enter. Look up at the ceiling paintings.
🌇
Five-storey pagoda at sunset (17:45) — the pagoda lit by the last of the sun, then by its own illumination. This is the moment. Stand at the southern end of the complex and look back at the full temple group against the sky.
🏘️
Backstreets after dark (18:15) — the quiet residential lanes east of the temple, or a short walk toward Sumida River (Skytree visible across the water, illuminated). A very different Asakusa from daytime.
💡 Dusk Asakusa is the connoisseur's timing. Most tour groups have gone home by 17:00; the evening locals arrive; the lantern lights make the buildings look older and more alive. You could come at 10am with 50,000 other tourists, or come now with a few hundred. The choice is obvious.

Omoide Yokocho — Memory Lane ⭐ Dinner¥8,000–12,000~A$82–124
Memory Lane is a narrow alley running under the Shinjuku elevated railway — ~20+ tiny yakitori bars, each seating 8–12 people, charcoal grills at eye level, smoke rising into the corrugated iron above. Built in the 1940s and essentially unchanged. The most atmospheric place to eat in Tokyo.

Walk the full alley first, both sides. Then pick the bar that looks most alive — busy, smoke rising, chef working fast. Sit at the counter. Point at skewers on the grill. Order cold Sapporo.
🍢 What to order: negima (chicken and leek) · tsukune (chicken meatball with egg yolk) · kawa (chicken skin, crispy) · reba (chicken liver, silky) · shishito peppers charred on the grill. 2–3 rounds of 4–5 skewers each, plus beer or highball. Budget ¥4,000–6,000pp at 2024+ pricing — ¥300–600 per skewer is realistic in the tourist-heavy stretches.
🔥 Fire damage note: Omoide Yokocho suffered a significant fire in November 2022 damaging ~10 bars; reconstruction has been ongoing. Most of the alley should be back by 2026, but verify on the night. If the atmosphere is disappointing or too tourist-heavy, Shinjuku Golden Gai is 10 min walk east — 200 tiny bars across six alleys, different vibe, many don't welcome tourists but the ones that do are excellent.
🌃 Omoide Yokocho is best experienced without a plan. Sit, eat, drink slowly, watch the Shinjuku night happen around you through the smoke. Trains pass overhead every few minutes. The noise, smoke, charcoal smell — this is Tokyo at its most itself.
